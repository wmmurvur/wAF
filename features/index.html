I

Never stops

Work keeps going through full contexts, full accounts and its own updates.

Read the district
  • Baton relay⑂ 92At the context level you set (40% by default), a session commits, writes a handover note and a fresh session carries on. No context rot.
  • Quota handoveracct-c 71%Before the 5-hour window fills, the next leg opens on the account with the most room.
  • Account policies4 built inFour built-in rules (@emptiest, @pool, @standing, @round-robin) or your own. A rule that can pick no account refuses the launch and says why.
  • Park and wake2 parksOne account or ten, when all of them are full the chain waits for the first reset and carries on by itself.
  • Ouroboros3.931.0An agent builds a new Agent Farm, restarts it inside VS Code and carries on in the same session. Nobody has to restart anything.
  • Sentryon watchAn agent runs the chain instead of a fixed rule: it takes the next item, opens a clean session, reads the result and decides what comes next.
  • Brakesbaton.stopA leg ceiling per chain, spend caps per workflow and one stop for a whole chain. Recorded, never deleted.

IV

Recipes and triggers

Work that starts on time, on an event, or from one sentence.

Read the district
  • Workflows8 savedA chain where each step hands its output to the next. A step can be an agent, an HTTP call, an image or video job, a browser run, or a stop that waits for your yes.
  • Schedules13 onDaily, weekly or once at a date and hour. A missed run is skipped, caught up, or put to you. With VS Code closed, a small service on Windows still runs them.
  • Watch triggers2 watchingA file lands in a folder and an agent wakes up. A live hook, or a post to a private address on your machine, works the same way.
  • GatekeepershadowBefore a trigger wakes an agent, free local checks and then Jev, a model that never chats and only decides, say whether there is real work. Off, watching in the shadow, or on.
  • Say it in wordsRuns tabType "run the filing batch on manager now". A cheap model turns it into a task, a schedule or a workflow, and nothing is set up until you press Create.
  • Queue and limits1/2 runningRuns wait their turn under ceilings, two at once and one per agent by default. A held run starts by itself as a slot frees.
  • A record of every run$0.12Every run writes what it said to a file, is marked failed when it crashed, and carries its price. A job you set up often becomes a template, and finished rows move to a monthly history.
  • Retries and rounds40 minA run that fails or hits a usage limit tries again by itself, after 40 minutes or at the plan's reset, even on another account. A big job runs in rounds inside one conversation.
  • One switchquiet hoursStop every automation at once, pause it for 1, 8 or 24 hours, or keep quiet hours in which nothing wakes unattended.
  • Forecast30 daysWhat your schedules will run over the next 7 days, each with its usual time and success rate from the last 30 days.

VIII

Just ask

You never have to learn the farm. Tell an agent, it sets it up.

Read the district
  • Just askin chatSay "make me a weekly video recipe" in a chat. The agent sets it up through the bridge; you never open a form.
  • 579 operationsfarm.opsWhat you can do with a click, an agent can do with a call: a button with no operation fails the build. A few things stay yours on purpose: granting a tool permission, stopping a question's countdown, and a secret's value. New abilities show up in farm.ops by themselves.
  • One command lineaf.jsThe bridge is a single shell command, so any agent that can run a command can use the farm. Gatekeeper also comes as an MCP server.
  • Agents with a homedutiesEach agent keeps its own identity, duties that run every turn or session, and tools. The Toolbox shows every skill, subagent, command, MCP server, hook and plugin on the machine, adds new ones and tests what an MCP server really offers.
  • Capability Matrix30 daysWhich agent has which skill, MCP server and memory file, and which of them were actually used in 30 days.
  • Phone appanywhereAnswer, approve with a button and watch the fleet from your pocket in the Agent Farm phone app, paired by a QR code; it can even wake a closed VS Code. Or a read-only web page on any device you allow.
  • Telegramone tapA Telegram chat talks to your agents. A card arrives when a turn ends or asks, and one tap answers it. Only the chats you allow, within a daily budget.
  • Sound10 tonesA tone for every event, for one agent or for one session: ten made for it and room for ten of your own, with quiet hours and a reminder for an unanswered question.
  • Read aloudaloudAn answer, a selection or the daily briefing read aloud, each in its own language, and whoever is asking named after the tone.
  • Take it with you.agentpackPack an agent as an .agentpack: its rules, tools, workflows and schedules, never its memory or secrets. Unpack it on another machine.
IX

Measured, not claimed

What every step cost, how much plan is left, and whether the work was good, in numbers.

Read the district
  • Live plan usage5h · weekHow full each account's five-hour window and week are, and when they reset, in the status bar and on the Dashboard. Every engine's limit apart.
  • Where it went9 kindsEvery turn split into nine kinds of step, from the model's thinking, billed as output, to local tools that cost nothing to run but make the next call bigger. Every figure is shown raw and weighted.
  • Budgets80% warnA daily budget per agent or for the whole fleet, a cap per workflow and per mission, a monthly media budget. A warning at 80%, an error past 100%.
  • Cheaper, not stopped→ haikuAs a window fills, automatic jobs wait for it to loosen, or move to a cheaper model. Sessions you opened by hand are left alone.
  • Cost alarmsmedianA day that costs far more than an agent's usual, or a week that jumps, raises a flag. The usual is the median of its real days, not a guess.
  • Honest numbersreal zerosCosts come from your own transcripts. A free model's zero is a real zero; a missing figure is shown as missing.
  • Judge laddercheap firstA check is judged by a cheap model first. Only a fail climbs to a stronger one, and a pass there overturns it, so no retry is paid for nothing.
  • EvalsscorecardSaved cases replayed after a change of model or instructions: accuracy, tests passed and what got worse, on a scorecard, not on how sure an agent sounds.
  • InsightsfindingsRepeated corrections turn into suggested rules. A finding is fixed, handed to its agent or dismissed, and whether the fix held is tracked.

Safe and yours alone

Guards stop what an agent should not do, and nothing about your work comes to us.

Read the district
  • On your machine127.0.0.1It runs inside VS Code and your work stays on your disk. The live hooks listen on 127.0.0.1 only.
  • No telemetry0 bytesNo analytics, no usage statistics: we don't know which features you use, how often or on what. Free never contacts us; a paid licence only checks its key.
  • ShellGuard4 rulesBefore a shell command runs, the shapes that garble or hide it are refused: non-ASCII, backticks, long heredocs, long quoted text. The agent is told the safe way: write the text to a file.
  • BridgeGuardto the bridgeAn agent that goes searching the disk for a session by its id is stopped and sent to the bridge, which reads it properly.
  • Permissions as shownenforcedThe permission a card shows is the one in force; if a policy forbids it, the session is refused, never quietly downgraded. A lint finds blanket allows and dangerous patterns.
  • Keys kept outscrubbedRoute keys are scrubbed from whatever a run prints, and Gatekeeper's log keeps only a hash and a length of what it read.
  • Browser on a leashallow listA browser step opens only the sites on its allow list, or fails without running, and asks you before each run unless you chose otherwise.
  • SuperpowersTDDEngineering disciplines on disk, such as tests first and systematic debugging, with quality gates.
