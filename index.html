    05 The rules of the relay
    
    
    
      
    Real defaults · every one is a setting
    Runs without you. Braked so it never runs away.
    
      
    Every number below is a setting you can change. These are the defaults the extension ships with.
    
    
    
    
    
      
    35%
    Converge
    The session is told once to start nothing new and finish the step in flight.
    contextConvergePct
    40%
    Hand over
    It stops, commits, writes its handover note and ends the run. The next leg opens with that note.
    contextHandoverPct
    90%
    Quota handover
    When its account's five-hour window is this full, the leg hands over and the next one opens on another account.
    quotaHandoverPct
    98%
    Weekly line
    The same handover for the weekly window, so a chain never runs an account into the wall.
    quotaWeeklyHandoverPct
    PARK
    Wait for the reset
    Every account past the wall? The next leg parks until the first reset, then wakes and carries on.
    parked until 02:15
    BRAKE
    Barren-chain brake
    Two legs in a row that end on the same commit stop the chain, instead of burning quota on nothing.
    batonMaxHops
    FAILSAFE
    Emergency leg
    A leg cut off by an error or left with uncommitted work gets a successor that is told what it left.
    batonEmergencyTemplate
    0
    No ceiling
    Chains are capped at 6 legs by default. Set it to 0 and the relay runs until the work is done.
    batonMaxHops: 0

    06 Backlog
    
    
    
      
    Projects of any kind · the work list · the track
    You write the work down. The fleet works it off.
    
      
    An app, a documentary, a thesis, a campaign: every project has its own page, and the work waits in one plain Backlog.md file. You put it in order; agents take the top of it, item by item, and tick off what they finish. The Track tab remembers how each one was done.

      • One line is enough. No code, no form. Ctrl+Alt+B takes a thought from anywhere in VS Code, and it is filed later, by you or by an agent sweeping.
        • 
        
      • The order is yours. Drag the queue. "Do the top five" means your top five, and anything waiting on an unfinished item is held back, with the reason shown.
        • 
        
      • Every session starts with what's next. The top of the queue rides in each session's brief, so a chain moves on to the next item with no paste and no lookup.
        • 
        
      • Big work comes in families. A whole and its parts, sized XS to XL. Below, one chain works through twelve parts, day and night.
        • 
        
      • Agents say what they wrote. #byAI is something an agent noticed and filed itself: a proposal. #byREQ is your thought, typed by an agent.
        • 
        
      • A plain file with a memory. Backlog.md opens in Obsidian and travels in git. The Track tab keeps which session, and which turn of it, worked each item, and what an agent tried that did not work, so the next one starts from there.

        17 Start
        
    
        
      
        Straight answers
        What it does, and where it stops.
        
      
        The limits are stated, not hidden. They follow from what the engines themselves offer.
        
    
        
    
        
      
        Where does the work come from?
        From each project's backlog: a plain Backlog.md you write and put in order. Every session starts with the top of the queue in its brief, agents tick off what they finish, and anything waiting on an unfinished item is held back.
        Which engines does it run?
        Claude Code, Codex, Gemini, Antigravity and OpenCode. A session, a task, a schedule, a workflow step or a mission delegation can run on any of them, and Agent Farm reads each CLI's own history on disk.
        Is the cost figure a real bill?
        Only Claude Code and OpenCode publish a price per turn. For the others a cost is derived from your pricing rules: good for comparing runs, not a bill. $0.00 is never printed where no price exists.
        Does every engine show up live, mid-turn?
        Claude Code and Codex report each step through their own hooks, and Agent Farm's hook relay forwards Gemini's, so their sessions reach the Live Timeline mid-turn. Antigravity and OpenCode have no hooks: Agent Farm follows the sessions it hosts for them. Only Claude Code can be told something back through a hook, so the context and quota lines reach its sessions; on the other engines a line that could not be said is recorded as undelivered.
        What happens when every account is out of quota?
        A leg hands over at 90% of its five-hour window and the next one opens on the emptiest account. When none is left, the chain parks until the first reset, then wakes and carries on.
        Can a chain run away?
        Chains stop after 6 legs by default. Set the limit to 0 for no ceiling. Two legs in a row that end on the same commit stop the chain either way.
        Does anything leave my machine?
        Nothing about your work comes to us. Free, Agent Farm never contacts us. A paid licence checks itself once a day: the key, a hash of the machine and the version, nothing else, and without a connection it keeps working for 30 days. There is no telemetry. Your engines talk to their own providers, exactly as they do without Agent Farm, and anything else it reaches is a service you switched on yourself.
